## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Five illustrations that look like siblings of Inbox zero and cost nothing in dependencies or theming.
- A first run that shows the app with real-looking data the user can discard in one tap, and a checklist that leaves when its job is done.
- A quiet, honest end-of-day state.
- One bar that searches, adds, commands and asks, without taking the quick-add line away from lists or surprising anyone who presses Enter.
- One toast that behaves the same after every action and never hides what the user is working on.

**Non-Goals:**
- Motion of any of the above (motion-v2 specifies the completion sequence, reflow and the toast's entrance).
- New colours, type or spacing (tokens-v2).
- Changing what Statistics counts beyond excluding sample tasks.
- A tour, coach marks or tooltips on first run.

## Decisions

**D1. Illustration briefs.** Five drawings, same rules as Inbox zero (one stroke width, round caps, `currentColor`, sunken fill, one accent shape at 15 %), each under 4 KB, named in `Illustration.tsx` and shown in `Illustrations.stories.tsx` in light and dark:

| Name | Where | Motif | Accent shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `today-clear` | Empty Today ("Nothing due today.") | A mug seen from the side with two steam lines, a small sun disc to the upper right | the sun |
| `week-clear` | Empty Upcoming ("Nothing scheduled yet.") | Seven small rounded squares in a row on a baseline, the first slightly raised | the raised square |
| `project-new` | A project with no open task | An open box with its lid tilted back and a pencil leaning on its edge | the pencil's cap |
| `day-done` | Done for today (Close the day) | A closed notebook with a ribbon, a crescent moon above it | the moon |
| `trash-empty` | Empty Trash | A bin with its lid resting on top and one leaf beside it | the leaf |

Alternatives: a commissioned illustration set (rejected: raster or multi-colour art cannot follow eight colour themes and dark mode, and costs bytes), or reusing icons at a large size (rejected: an icon at 160 px reads as a mistake, not a moment). Why these motifs: each names the state in one glance without text, and none carries a face, a person or a mascot, which would date and would need localisation of tone.

**D2. Sample tasks, not a sample project.** The six-task project needed a project to be trashed and taught six things at once; three Inbox tasks teach one thing each and leave in one tap. The tasks (English, translated through the catalog): "Tick me off when you have read this" (notes: three sentences on the panel, the Add menu and Undo), "Plan today: this one is due at 10:00 and takes 15 minutes" (due today 10:00, estimate 15 m), "Open me: I have a subtask" (one subtask "Subtasks tick off on their own row"). No label is created, so nothing is left behind. They are created as ordinary ops on the device when the user chooses "Start with three sample tasks" on the guide's first step, so they sync like any task and can be edited, completed or trashed by hand; "Remove sample tasks" trashes every task still marked `sample` as one batch with the usual Undo toast. Alternative: creating them automatically for every new account (rejected: it writes into an account nobody asked to fill, and a member joining a shared server would get them too). Alternative: keeping "Show me around" as well (rejected: two offers on one step is one too many; the richer walkthrough belongs in the user guide).

**D3. The `sample` marker is an additive task field.** `task.sample` (boolean, default false, whole value, not content, never encrypted) in `internal/sync/ops.go` `Schema`, `web/src/lib/types.ts` and the defaults in `web/src/lib/ops.ts`. Statistics ("Excluded rows"), the weekly digest and the Loop pulse skip rows with `sample` true, open or completed, through the one shared predicate that already skips trashed rows, and the shared fixture gains a sample row so the device and the server agree. Completing a sample task keeps the marker, so a tried-out tick never inflates a week. Alternative: a "Sample" label (rejected: the user may delete the label or put it on real tasks, and a label is content under encryption). Alternative: a title prefix (rejected: fragile and visible). Older servers reject the op as an unknown field and the device keeps its local value, as for every additive field; older clients show the tasks as ordinary tasks, which is harmless.

**D4. Showing the marker.** A sample task's row shows the muted word "Sample" in its metadata line (Calm and Detailed alike; it is a fact, not a chip), and its panel shows a one-line notice "A sample task · Remove sample tasks" under the title. The word counts as one of the row's visible properties, only on sample tasks, so no real task gets closer to the budget of seven. "Remove sample tasks" is in the panel notice, in the Inbox header's overflow while any sample task exists, and in the first-day checklist.

**D5. The first-day checklist.** Three steps, each verified from local data, nothing synced for the ticks: "Add a task" (any non-sample task exists), "Plan today" (any task has a due date set by the user, or Today was opened after the first task), "Get it on another device" (the app is installed on this device, or the account's sync state shows a second device). The checklist is the start guide's last view ("Your first day"), shown after step three, and reachable afterwards from a "Start guide · 1 of 3 done" entry in the sidebar's More group, under the other secondary destinations, with a "Hide" in its menu. The entry disappears when all three are done or when hidden; `user_prefs.start_checklist` (`hidden`, additive, synced) records the hide so the phone does not show it again. Alternative: a banner above the list (rejected by the interface budget: nothing between the header and the list). Alternative: a sidebar footer chip (rejected: the footer is one row and already holds the account, the assistant, the bell, the sync dot and the collapse control). Why More: it is not a primary destination, it is where secondary things already live, and an entry that leaves when done costs nothing afterwards.

**D6. Done for today.** After the close writes, the Close the day screen replaces its step card with one state: the `day-done` illustration, "Day closed, 18:42", one line with the figures the first step computed ("5 done, 3 moved to tomorrow, 1 kept"), the line for tomorrow quoted when there is one, a primary "Back to Today" and a secondary "Reopen the day" (ux-feedback-2). No streak, score, confetti or comparison. The state is device interface state: nothing is written to show it, and reloading the route shows the ordinary first step with "Day closed" in its header, as today. This supersedes the "then return to the Today view" clause of close-the-day's "Closing the day" requirement; because that change is still open, this change ADDS the "Done for today" requirement and task 4.4 amends that one clause once close-the-day is archived. Reduced motion: the state appears with a crossfade only.

**D7. One bar, four modes, explicit prefixes.** `Ctrl`/`⌘`+`K` keeps opening the palette in Search mode, so nobody's muscle memory breaks. A segmented control under the field (Search, Add, Commands, Ask) and three prefixes switch modes: `+` to Add, `>` to Commands, `?` to Ask; typing a prefix as the first character switches and removes it from the text. Add mode is the quick add inside the bar with the view's defaults of the page it was opened from, the inline highlighting of D8, the chips row and the "→ title" preview; Enter adds and keeps the bar open with an empty field, as the list line does; Escape closes. Commands mode lists the registry commands only, as the palette's command group does today. Ask mode is shown only when the assistant is available to the user; Enter hands the text to the assistant panel as one turn, exactly like the Ask composer. Search mode's last entry is always "Add '<text>' as a task", and `Ctrl`/`⌘`+`Enter` activates it without moving to it; it is never first, so Enter on a search never creates a task by surprise. `q` on a screen with no quick add of its own (Settings, Calendar's Day and Week, Statistics, the review screens, Labels, Trash) opens the bar in Add mode with the Inbox's defaults; on list screens `q` keeps focusing the line. Alternatives: making Add the default mode (rejected: it breaks search-by-Ctrl+K, the palette's whole purpose); showing "Add" as the first entry when nothing matches (rejected: the first entry would change meaning with the data); replacing the list's quick-add line with the bar (rejected: the line carries view-aware defaults, is one of the two allowed chrome rows, and is where touch users add).

**D8. Inline highlighting.** The quick add stays a plain `<input>` for accessibility and for every existing test; a highlight layer behind it, in the same font and padding, mirrors the text and tints each recognised span with the token's soft colour (the chip colours already used by `ParseChips`), scrolling with the field. The chips row stays as the explanation and the place to dismiss a token; a dismissed span loses its tint. The layer is `aria-hidden`; screen readers keep the chips' announcement. Alternative: a contenteditable field with real inline chips (rejected: a second input model, IME and selection bugs, every quick add test rewritten). Under high contrast the tint is a 1 px underline in the token colour.

**D9. The toast primitive.** One toast at a time, replacing the previous; one sentence that names what happened ("3 tasks rescheduled", "Moved to Home"); at most two controls, Undo first; five seconds after one task's action, ten after a batch; the timer pauses while the pointer is over the toast or focus is inside it and restarts on leave; Tab reaches Undo, `Ctrl`/`⌘`+`Z` outside a text field undoes while it shows; focus is never moved into the toast on its own; `role="status"` in the existing polite region. Never covering the focused element (WCAG 2.2, 2.4.11): while a toast is shown, the content column's scroll padding at the bottom grows by the toast's height so a focused row or field scrolls above it, and from 640 px up the toast sits bottom right outside the list column, so a focused element is behind it only when the panel is open and wider than the region, in which case the toast moves to bottom left. Reduced motion: opacity only (motion-v2 owns the entrance otherwise). Alternative: an auto-focused toast (rejected: it steals the keyboard from the list). Alternative: stacking toasts (rejected: two Undo controls are ambiguous).

**D10. Spec placement.** `first-run` "Three-step first run" is modified by lean-chrome, so the sample offer and the checklist are ADDED requirements that name the guide's steps; the old "Sample project" requirement is REMOVED with its migration. `design-system` "Illustrations" was added by inbox-means-unplaced, so the set is an ADDED "Illustration set" requirement; the toast is an ADDED "Toast primitive". `weekly-review` "Closing the day" is added by close-the-day, so "Done for today" is ADDED and D6's one-clause amendment waits for the archive. `command-palette` "Open and close the palette" and `natural-language-capture` "Live preview chips" are modified in full here; no open change touches either (`focus-mode` modifies "Commands by name", `time-tracking` "Attribute tokens"). `statistics` "Excluded rows" is modified in full; `stats-dashboard` and `time-tracking` modify other requirements of that spec. The Loop pulse requirement is modified by `loop-home-refresh`, so the pulse's exclusion is stated in the first-run requirement and implemented through the shared predicate, not as an open-loops delta.

**D11. Interface budget.** No new row above any list: the checklist is a sheet view and a More entry, the sample notice is inside the panel, the bar is an overlay. Visible task properties: the "Sample" word only on sample tasks. Primary sidebar destinations unchanged. Scroll regions unchanged (the bar is a dialog with one scroller). Shortcuts: `q` gains scope on screens that had none, registered as the existing `list.quick-add` entry with a wider scope so the registry test still sees one key; the `+`, `>` and `?` prefixes are characters typed into the bar, not shortcuts, so nothing can conflict; `Ctrl`/`⌘`+`Enter` inside the bar is local to the dialog.

**D12. Size and memory.** Five SVGs at under 4 KB each, about 18 KB raw and 5 KB gzipped, in the illustrations module the empty-state pattern already imports; the highlight layer and the checklist together about 3 KB gzipped; the bar's modes live in the palette's lazy chunk except Add mode's quick add, which is already in the main bundle. Measured in task 7.5. Server: one schema entry and one predicate; no memory change.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [The `+`, `>` and `?` prefixes collide with a search for text starting with those characters] → a prefix only switches mode as the first character of an empty field; a second press, or `Esc`, returns to Search with the character kept; the hint line names the three prefixes while the field is empty.
- [Sample tasks reach other devices before the user removes them] → they are ordinary tasks and the removal is one batch that syncs too; the marker keeps them out of every count.
- [A translation of the sample titles is longer than the row] → the titles are at most 50 characters in every catalog, checked by the i18n test.
- [The checklist's "second device" step is never satisfiable for a single-device user] → "Hide" is always offered, and installing the app on the same device satisfies it.
- [Scroll padding for the toast shifts the list while the user reads] → the padding is added once when the toast shows and removed when it hides; the list does not scroll unless the focused element would be behind the toast.
- [Done for today delays the return to Today by one click] → "Back to Today" is the primary action and has focus; Escape does the same.

## Migration Plan

Additive: `task.sample` and `user_prefs.start_checklist` are ignored by older clients and rejected by older servers as unknown fields, as every additive field is. Accounts that already created the "Show me around" project keep it as an ordinary project; no data is touched. No new environment variable, no migration of rows (fields materialise from the op log). Rollback: older builds ignore both fields.
