# Feedback and support

OpenTodo is free and open source, made by one person in the open. These are the ways to reach that person and to help the project.

## Report a problem or suggest an idea

[Open an issue on GitHub](https://github.com/OpenTodoApp/OpenTodo/issues). Say what you did, what you expected and what happened instead, and which version you run (the Help page shows it at the top). Every report is read. Before reporting a problem, the [troubleshooting guide](troubleshooting.md) may already have the fix.

**Something isn't working** (in the account menu under Help, on the phone in the drawer's account row, and in **Settings → About → Feedback and problems**) opens a short form: write what happened, and a hello is welcome too. Below your words it adds a few lines the app already knows: the server's version, the page you were on, your browser's name, the language and whether you were offline. You can edit or delete those lines before you send. Nothing is sent by the app itself. **Open on GitHub** opens a new issue with your text, in your browser; **Send by email** opens a message to the project's feedback address, info@open-todo.app. When the text is too long for a link, it is copied instead, and the dialog says so so you can paste it.

If you found a security problem, do not open a public issue: follow `SECURITY.md` in the repository instead.

If something stops you because of how you use a computer or phone (a screen reader, the keyboard only, voice control, zoom), add the label `accessibility`: such a report is answered within 10 business days. The [accessibility statement](accessibility.md) says what is tested and what is known.

## Talk to other people

[Join the OpenTodo server on Discord](https://discord.gg/mmSsuCRduD) to ask a question, show how you use the app or see what other people build. It is the place for conversation; a bug report is still best as an issue, so it is not lost in the chat. Nothing in the app talks to Discord: the link opens it in a new tab only when you choose to.

## Follow the news

**Settings → About → What's new** shows, inside the app, a short note for each release: the newest in full, earlier ones behind *Earlier versions*. The notes ship with the version your server runs; the app never fetches them. The notes are in English. A dot appears next to About when your server runs a version whose notes you have not opened yet on this device; **Version notices** turns the dot off, and the notes stay.

[OpenTodo on X](https://x.com/opentodoapp) posts each release and the work in progress in a few lines. Follow it if you want to know what changed without reading the changelog; the longer version is always on the [blog](https://open-todo.app/blog/). As with Discord, nothing in the app talks to X: the link opens it in a new tab only when you choose to.

## Help other people find it

[Star OpenTodo on GitHub](https://github.com/OpenTodoApp/OpenTodo). Stars are how many people discover open-source projects, so a star is a real help. Telling a friend who pays for a task app works too.

## Buy me a coffee

If OpenTodo saves you a subscription, [a coffee](https://buymeacoffee.com/pixari) helps keep it going. It is entirely optional: OpenTodo has no paid plan and every feature is free.

## Settings → About

**Settings → About** has all of these in one place: the version your server runs, Help, the documentation and the website, the issue tracker, the Discord server, the X account as Follow on X, and What's new, the source code and the coffee button.

## The welcome note

The first time the owner opens a new server, a short note thanks them and shows these links. It appears once per account; closing it on one device closes it on all of them. People invited to the server do not see it, and neither does the public demo. The owner can open it again from **Settings → About → Welcome note**.
