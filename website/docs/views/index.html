Views

A view is a way of looking at your tasks. OpenTodo comes with a handful, and you can save your own. Every view is built from the copy of your tasks on your device, so they all work offline and update the moment something changes.

The built-in views#

They're at the top of the sidebar. Each has a shortcut: press g, then the letter.

View Shows Shortcut
Inbox Tasks that aren't in a project yet g i
Today Tasks due today, with overdue ones first g t
Upcoming Tasks due after today, grouped by day g u
Assigned to me Open tasks assigned to you, across projects g a
Day and Week Your timed tasks on a calendar g d, g w
Completed What you finished most recently g c
Filter Any search you can describe g f
Trash Deleted tasks and projects g x
Activity What changed in your shared projects g h

The numbers next to the names count open tasks.

Plan your day with Today#

Today is where most people start. Overdue tasks sit in their own group at the top, in red, so nothing slips. Below them are today's tasks, sorted by time.

Anything you add here with quick add is due today, unless you type another date. To push a task to later, open it and change Due, or press s to place it on the Day calendar.

Plan your week with Upcoming#

Upcoming lists everything that has a date after today, under headings like Tomorrow and the weekday names. It's a good place for a short weekly review: scan the next few days, move what won't fit, and spot empty days.

When you want to see the hours, switch to Week, which shows your timed tasks as blocks on a grid. See Time blocking.

Narrow a list by duration#

Above most lists are four buttons: Quick wins (15 minutes or less), Under 30 min, Over 1 h and Unestimated. Turn one on to see only matching tasks; select it again to clear it. Your choice is remembered on that device. Estimates are set with e or in the task panel.

Save your own views#

When the built-in views aren't enough, describe what you want in the Filter view, then save it. For example, @waiting & open lists everything you're waiting on, and due <= 7 days & p1 shows urgent tasks for the coming week. The filter language is explained in Labels and filters.

To save the current filter, press s in the results (or use the save button), and give it a name. Saved views are pinned to the Views group in the sidebar with a live count, and they sync to all your devices.

Each saved view can group its tasks (by project, date, priority or label) and sort them (by date, priority, your own order, creation time or title). Change these from the top of the view. To change the query itself, press /, edit it and press Enter.

The All link next to Views (or g v) opens the list of all your saved views. From there you can rename, reorder, unpin or delete them.

Build a dashboard#

A dashboard puts up to six saved views side by side, each showing its count and first ten tasks. It's handy for a morning review: "Due this week", "Deep work" and "Waiting on others" on one screen.

Open the views list (g v), choose + New dashboard, name it, then add saved views as panels from the dashboard's header. On a phone the panels stack. Move between panels with Tab, and through a panel's tasks with j / k.

Choose where the app opens#

Each device can open on a different view: your laptop on a "Work" view, your phone on Today. Pick it in Settings → Start view, or from a saved view's menu with Set as start view on this device. If that view is deleted later, the device opens on the Inbox and tells you why.

Tips#

  • Subtasks show up on their own in Today and Upcoming, with their parent's name next to them, so you see exactly what's due.
  • Views that sort by date (Today, Upcoming, labels, filters) can't be reordered by hand. Projects and the Inbox can.
  • Filter queries has ten ready-made examples to copy.
